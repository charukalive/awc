# Citizen Legal Action Guide & Animal Law FAQs
## Public-Facing Legal Knowledge Base for AWC Website

This guide translates Sri Lanka's animal protection statutes and court rulings into clear, actionable answers for citizens, animal caregivers, and police officers.

---

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

#### Q1: Can a police officer refuse to accept an animal cruelty complaint?
**No.** Under **Section 12 of the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Ordinance No. 13 of 1907**, all cruelty offences are **cognizable offences**. This means:
1. Police officers are legally mandated to record your complaint (in the Crime Information Book - CIB).
2. Police officers possess the statutory power to **arrest the accused person without a warrant**.
3. If an officer hesitates, respectfully draw their attention to **Section 12 of Ordinance No. 13 of 1907** and request the Complaint Reference Number (CIB Entry #).

---

#### Q2: What is the legal time limit to report an act of animal cruelty?
**Crucial Warning:** Under **Section 8 of the 1907 Ordinance**, legal proceedings must be instituted in court **within THREE (03) MONTHS** from the date the offence was committed. If you wait more than 3 months, the case will be statute-barred and dismissed by the court. Always gather evidence and file complaints immediately.

---

#### Q3: Can an owner beat, starve, or abandon their sick or old dog or pet?
**No.** 
- **Section 2 & 3 (1907 Ordinance)**: Makes it a criminal offence to beat, starve, neglect, or cause unnecessary pain to any captive or domestic animal.
- **Section 7 (1907 Ordinance)**: Makes it an offence to abandon a diseased, injured, or dying animal on any street or public road.
- **Jayasinghe v. Silva (1921)**: The Supreme Court held that animal owners have an **absolute and non-delegable duty** to actively supervise the welfare of their animals. Giving verbal orders to servants or workers does not absolve the owner of criminal liability.

---

#### Q4: A neighbour's cow or dog entered my private land. Can I shoot, poison, or beat it?
**Absolutely not.** 
- In **Mendis v. Hunucumbura (1928) 29 NLR 380**, the Supreme Court ruled that shooting or injuring a trespassing animal when peaceful expulsion was possible constitutes **causing unnecessary pain** under Section 4(1)(b) of the 1907 Ordinance.
- Poisoning or killing an owned animal also constitutes criminal **Mischief under Sections 411 and 412 of the Penal Code**, punishable by up to **2 to 5 years imprisonment**.
- You may only lawfully lead the trespassing animal to a local council pound; you have zero legal right to inflict injury or death.

---

#### Q5: What documents are required by law to transport cattle, goats, or pigs in a truck?
Under the **Animals Act No. 29 of 1958**, **Gazette No. 1629/17 (2009)**, and the **Animals (Amendment) Act No. 19 of 2026**, transporting cattle, buffalo, goats, sheep, or pigs across Divisional Secretariat boundaries requires:
1. **Transport Permit (Schedule 01)** issued by the Divisional Secretary.
2. **Health Certificate (Schedule 03)** issued by the Government Veterinary Surgeon (GVS) within 24 hours of travel.
3. **Vehicle Fitness Certificate (Schedule 04 & 05)** verifying the lorry has proper ventilation, headroom, and a **loading ramp**.
4. **Cattle Vouchers (Schedule 06)** with matching official ear tag numbers.
5. **Mandatory Signboard**: The front of the lorry must display a **30" x 4"** white signboard with reflective red lettering reading **"සතුන් ප්‍රවාහනය කිරීම" / "Transport of Animals"**.

*Failure to possess these allows police to seize both the animals and the transport vehicle (Nizar v. SI Kirinda CA 861/85).*

---

#### Q6: Can a court return rescued animals back to an abusive owner or illegal transporter?
**No.** 
- In **SC Appeal No. 100/2025 (*Justice for Animals and Nature v. OIC Udawalawa Police*)**, the Supreme Court overturned a Magistrate's order that had released cruelly transported buffaloes back to the convicted drivers. The Supreme Court ruled that releasing animals back to individuals who admitted cruelty directly undermines the protective purpose of animal welfare legislation.
- Furthermore, under **Badulla High Court Rev. 41/2017**, Magistrates have the authority to grant temporary custody of rescued animals to a recognized Animal Welfare Organization on an indemnity bond pending trial.

---

#### Q7: Does an Animal Welfare NGO have legal standing (Locus Standi) in court?
**Yes.** In **CA (PHC) APN 22/2018 (*Irangani De Silva v. OIC Ella*)**, the Court of Appeal confirmed that animal welfare organizations have recognized legal standing (*locus standi*) to intervene in court proceedings as aggrieved parties to ensure the welfare and custody of victimized animals are safeguarded.

---

#### Q8: Is it legal to slaughter a pregnant or milking cow in Sri Lanka?
**Strictly illegal.** 
Under the **Butchers (Amendment) Act No. 13 of 2008 (amending Section 18 of Butchers Ordinance No. 9 of 1893)**:
- It is an absolute criminal offence to slaughter any **pregnant cow** or **lactating (milking) cow**, even if the slaughterhouse has a general license.
- Penalty: Mandatory fine of **Rs. 50,000** and imprisonment up to **3 years**.

---

#### Q9: What are the legal penalties if a person's aggressive dog attacks a person or animal?
Under the **Dangerous Animals (Amendment) Act No. 40 of 2022**:
- The maximum penalty for an owner who negligently fails to control or restrain a dangerous animal was increased from Rs. 1,000 to **Rs. 250,000**.
- The court may also order the forfeiture or secure retention of the animal.

---

#### Q10: What does the new Animal Welfare Bill (2026) change?
The new Bill gazetted in July 2026:
1. Replaces the Rs. 100 fine with penalties up to **Rs. 500,000** and up to **3 years imprisonment**.
2. Introduces a legal **Duty of Care** holding pet owners, farmers, and breeders accountable for chronic neglect (failure to provide food, water, medical care, or shelter).
3. Establishes the **National Animal Welfare Authority (NAWA)** to regulate pet shops, dog breeding, animal transport, and cruelty enforcement.

---

#### Q11: What is a "B-Report" (B-වාර්තාව) and why is it important?
Under Sections 115 and 136(1)(b) of the Code of Criminal Procedure Act, a **B-Report** is the formal written report that police submit to the Magistrate to initiate a criminal court case.
* The moment police file the B-Report, the court generates an official **Magistrate's Court Case Number** (e.g. `B/1234/2026`).
* Once a B-Report is filed, lawyers representing the complainant, pet owner, or an animal welfare organization (like AWC) can file a proxy and motion to intervene, represent the animal's welfare interests, and ensure evidence is properly evaluated.

---

#### Q12: Why can't police prosecute based solely on viral social media posts without witness statements?
Under Sri Lankan criminal procedure (Section 110 of the CCPA), guilt must be proven beyond reasonable doubt. Social media videos or hearsay are not sufficient on their own without direct witness testimony. An eyewitness statement provides sworn, direct evidence about what was seen, heard, and experienced first-hand, identifying the suspect and giving the prosecution solid ground to stand in court.

---

#### Q13: How do I submit smartphone video or CCTV evidence so the court will accept it?
Under the **Evidence (Special Provisions) Act, No. 14 of 1995**, electronic recordings must follow the **3-CD & Affidavit Rule**:
1. **Three (03) identical Compact Discs (CD-R/DVD-R):**
   * Disc 1: For the Court record (Magistrate).
   * Disc 2: For the Prosecution (Police / State Counsel).
   * Disc 3: For the Defense (Accused's counsel).
2. **Sworn Affidavit (දිවුරුම් ප්‍රකාශය):** Signed before a Justice of the Peace (JP) by the person who recorded the footage, stating the smartphone/CCTV make, model, exact date, time, and location, and swearing that the files are exact, unedited, and unaltered replicas of the original recording.

---

#### Q14: What is the difference between a Notice, a Summons, and a Warrant?
* **Notice (දැන්වීම):** An administrative request by police to attend a station for an inquiry.
* **Summons (සිතාසිය):** A formal judicial order signed by a Magistrate commanding a person to appear in court on a specific date. Ignoring a summons leads to an immediate arrest warrant.
* **Warrant of Arrest (අත්අඩංගුවට ගැනීමේ වරෙන්තුව):** A judicial order directing police to arrest the named person anywhere in Sri Lanka and bring them before court.
* **Search Warrant (සෝදිසි වරෙන්තුව):** A court order authorizing police to enter private premises to search for cruelty evidence, weapons, or captive animals.

---

#### Q15: What is a "Court Exhibit / Production" (නඩු භාණ්ඩ) and what happens to seized live animals?
Under Section 425 of the CCPA, physical evidence (weapons, vehicles used in illegal transport, or victim animals) seized by police are produced in court as exhibits. Because living animals cannot be held in police evidence rooms, animal welfare lawyers move the court under **Badulla High Court Rev. 41/2017** to hand over temporary care and custody of the animals to a recognized welfare sanctuary on an indemnity bond, preventing them from being returned to the abuser (*SC Appeal No. 100/2025*).

---

#### Q16: How can citizens use the Right to Information (RTI) Act to investigate animal cases?
Under the **Right to Information Act No. 12 of 2016**:
1. Submit **Form RTI 01** to the Information Officer (IO) of the relevant public authority (Police Station, DAPH, DWC, or Local Council).
2. You can request certified copies of Police Information Book (IB) complaint entries, B-Reports, veterinary post-mortem reports, butcher licenses, and sterilization budget expenditures.
3. The IO must respond within **14 working days**. If denied, you can file a First Appeal to the Designated Officer (DO) and a Second Appeal to the **Right to Information Commission (RTIC)** in Colombo.

### Step-by-Step Citizen Protocol: When Witnessing Cruelty

```text
Step 1: Prioritize Personal Safety
• Never confront armed or aggressive individuals directly.
• Never trespass onto private land to retrieve an animal without police escort.

Step 2: Collect Verifiable Evidence
• Photographs & Videos: Clear footage showing the animal's physical distress, wounds, living conditions, or the act of cruelty.
• Identifiers: Record vehicle license plates, driver/owner name, exact street location, landmark, and GPS coordinates.
• Eyewitnesses: Note names and phone numbers of willing witnesses.

Step 3: Dial Emergency Hotlines (If Active Danger)
• Sri Lanka Police Emergency: Dial 119
• Department of Wildlife Conservation (Wild Animals/Elephants): +94 11 2 888 585
• WHO Rabies First Aid: If bitten, immediately wash wound with soap and running water for 15 minutes, then visit the nearest government hospital.

Step 4: Lodge a Formal Complaint at the Police Station
• Request to meet the OIC (Officer-in-Charge) or Crime Branch OIC.
• Submit a written complaint detailing the date, time, location, suspect details, and witness accounts.
• Cite: Section 2, 3, 4, or 5 of the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Ordinance No. 13 of 1907.
• Request the CIB Entry Number (Crime Information Book #).

Step 5: Submit a Case Dossier to AWC
• Submit the evidence and CIB number via the AWC Incident Portal.
• AWC legal coordinators can review the facts, link with volunteer lawyers, and support Magistrate Court follow-through.
```

### 9. Special Advisory: Veterinary Medical Negligence Protocol
*(පශු වෛද්‍ය නොසැලකිලිමත්කම / கால்நடை மருத்துவ அலட்சியம்)*

If the complaint relates to veterinary surgical malpractice, clinical negligence, or unauthorized veterinary practice:

1. **Direct Scope Notice:** Direct veterinary clinical malpractice falls under the statutory regulatory jurisdiction of the **Sri Lanka Veterinary Council (SLVC)**, established under the Veterinary Surgeons and Practitioners Act No. 46 of 1956. It is outside AWC's direct casework scope.
2. **Step 1 - File with SLVC:** Send a formal written complaint with medical treatment slips, prescriptions, and veterinary bills directly to the Registrar of the Sri Lanka Veterinary Council via email:  
   👉 **`registrarslvetcouncil@gmail.com`**
3. **Step 2 - Lodge a Police Complaint:** File a formal Information Book entry at your local police station if fraud, unauthorized surgery, or criminal negligence is suspected.
4. **Step 3 - Forward to AWC:** If no official response or inquiry is conducted by the authorities after submitting to SLVC and the Police, you may forward your reference numbers and case dossier to **`awcsrilanka@gmail.com`** for guidance.
