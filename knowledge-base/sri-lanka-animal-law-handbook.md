### 1. Overview & Jurisprudential Foundation

Sri Lanka's legal architecture for animal protection is currently in a state of historic transition. For over a century, the primary statutory weapon against cruelty has been the **Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Ordinance No. 13 of 1907**, an antiquated colonial statute that views cruelty purely through negative prohibitions rather than positive affirmative welfare standards, with penalties capped at a negligible Rs. 100.

However, alongside the 1907 Ordinance, Sri Lankan animal law is woven from a patchwork of specialized statutes:
- **Agrarian & Livestock Control**: Regulated by the *Animals Act No. 29 of 1958* and *Gazette No. 1629/17*, governing cattle branding, vouchers, transport permits, and illegal slaughter.
- **Wildlife & Elephants**: Regulated under the *Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance No. 2 of 1937 (FFPO)*.
- **Public Health & Disease**: Controlled by the *Rabies Ordinance*, *Dogs Registration Ordinance*, and *Animal Diseases Act*.
- **Public Safety**: Regulated under the *Dangerous Animals (Amendment) Act No. 40 of 2022*.
- **Reform Landmark**: The **Animal Welfare Bill (Gazetted July 2026)**, introducing a statutory **Duty of Care**, a **National Animal Welfare Authority**, and fines up to **Rs. 500,000** and imprisonment up to **3 years**.

#### 2.1 Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Ordinance No. 13 of 1907
*(Chapter 573, amended up to Act No. 22 of 1955; read with Judicature Act s. 33)*

* **Scope & Definition (Section 14)**:
  * "Animal" means any domestic or captured animal, including any bird, fish, or reptile in a state of captivity or human possession.
  * "Street" includes any way, road, lane, square, court, alley, passage, or open space accessible to the public.
* **Key Prohibited Acts**:
  * **Section 2(1)(a)**: Cruelly beating, kicking, ill-treating, overloading, overriding, torturing, or causing unnecessary pain/suffering.
  * **Section 2(1)(b)**: Any act or omission causing unnecessary pain or suffering.
  * **Section 2(1)(c)**: Conveying, carrying, or transporting any animal in any vehicle, boat, crate, or basket in such a manner or position as to subject it to unnecessary pain or suffering.
  * **Section 3**: Starvation, mutilation, or severe neglect resulting in suffering; failure by owner/manager to provide basic care.
  * **Section 4**: Unlawfully and cruelly killing any animal.
  * **Section 5**: Employing or working an animal that is unfit due to disease, infirmity, wound, or exhaustion.
  * **Section 7**: Abandoning a diseased or disabled animal in any street or public place to die.
* **Enforcement Powers & Critical Procedural Rules**:
  * **Section 12 (COGNIZABLE OFFENCE)**: Every offence under this Ordinance is a **cognizable offence** within the meaning of the Code of Criminal Procedure. **Police officers can arrest offenders WITHOUT A WARRANT.**
  * **Section 8 (STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS)**: Prosecutions must be instituted **within THREE (03) MONTHS** of the date of the alleged offence. Reports after 3 months are legally barred under the 1907 Act!
  * **Section 9**: Power of Magistrates, SPs/ASPs, Primary Court Judges, and AGAs to order the destruction (humane euthanasia) of hopelessly suffering or mortally injured animals.
  * **Section 13**: Power of officers to seize and detain the animal as case production; maintenance costs recoverable from the convicted owner like a fine.
* **Current Penalty**:
  * Fine up to Rs. 100/= (Section 2, 3, 4, 5, 7) or up to 3 months (or 6 months under Sec 4 & 5) imprisonment (rigorous or simple).

#### 2.2 Animals Act No. 29 of 1958 & Extraordinary Gazette No. 1629/17 (2009.11.26)
This statute regulates the national cattle population, prevents cattle rustling, and sets strict rules for animal transportation and slaughter.

* **Cattle Identification & Ownership (Section 8)**:
  * All cattle/buffalo over 18 months must be registered, ear-tagged, and possess a **Cattle Voucher (Schedule 06)** signed before the Government Veterinary Surgeon (GVS). A Grama Niladhari certificate is *not* legally sufficient to establish cattle ownership.
* **Mandatory Transport Documents (Gazette 1629/17)**:
  Any transport of cattle/buffalo across Divisional Secretariat boundaries requires four mandatory documents:
  1. **Transport Permit (Schedule 01)**: Issued by the Divisional Secretary (DS).
  2. **Health Certificate (Schedule 03)**: Issued by the Government Veterinary Surgeon within 24 hours of travel, verifying freedom from FMD, BQ, and HS.
  3. **Vehicle Fitness Certificate (Schedule 04 & 05)**: Issued by the GVS certifying that the vehicle has sufficient ventilation, headroom, and floor space.
  4. **Cattle Voucher (Schedule 06)**: Demonstrating lawful ownership.
* **Vehicle Physical Requirements**:
  * Must be fitted with a proper **Loading Ramp** so animals are not dropped or dragged.
  * Must display a white board measuring **30 inches × 4 inches** on the front with bold red lettering: **"සතුන් ප්‍රවාහනය කිරීම" / "Transport of Animals"**.
  * Return requirement: Transport permits must be surrendered back to the issuing DS within 3 days of expiration.
* **Illegal Cattle Slaughter Restrictions**:
  * Strictly bans the slaughter of **cows under 12 years**, **pregnant cows**, and **lactating cows**.
  * Animals can only be slaughtered if certified permanently infertile or unfit for agricultural labour by an authorized GVS.

#### 2.3 Animals (Amendment) Act No. 19 of 2026
* **Expansion to Small Ruminants & Pigs**:
  * Amends Section 38 of the Animals Act to explicitly expand the statutory definition of "animal" to include **Goats, Sheep, and Pigs**.
  * Mandatory transport permits, vehicle inspection, health certification, and movement tracking now legally apply to the transport of pigs, goats, and sheep.
  * Purpose: Contain zoonotic diseases and prevent horrific transport cruelty in the meat trade.

#### 2.4 Butchers Ordinance No. 9 of 1893 (Amendments: 2005 No. 12, 2008 No. 13)
* **Licensing Requirement (Sections 4-11)**:
  * No person can practice as a butcher without an annual license issued by the "Proper Authority" (Municipal Mayor, Urban Council Chairman, or Pradeshiya Sabha Chairman). License expires December 31st each year.
* **Pre-Slaughter Protocol & 48-Hour Notice (Section 21)**:
  * 48 hours prior notice must be given to the Proper Authority/Public Health Inspector (PHI) before slaughtering any cattle.
  * Cattle must be exposed to public view for 24 hours prior to slaughter to detect stolen animals.
  * Ante-mortem inspection by a PHI/GVS is mandatory. Sick, emaciated, or dying animals cannot be slaughtered.
* **2008 Amendment (Act No. 13 of 2008) - Pregnant & Lactating Cows**:
  * Sections 18(1) and 20(2) strictly prohibit the slaughter of any **pregnant cow** or **lactating cow**, with or without a permit.
  * Violation is a serious criminal offence carrying a fine of **Rs. 50,000** and imprisonment up to **3 years**.

#### 2.5 Dangerous Animals Ordinance No. 38 of 1921 (Amended by Act No. 40 of 2022)
* Originally Chapter 49.
* Under the **2022 Amendment (Act No. 40 of 2022, certified 17 Nov 2022)**:
  * Section 7(4)(a) penalties for owners who negligently fail to safeguard the public from dangerous animals (e.g., ferocious dogs attacking neighbours/pedestrians) were drastically increased from Rs. 1,000 to **Rs. 250,000**!
  * Gives Magistrates robust powers to order the seizure, secure retention, or humane destruction of ferocious animals whose owners fail in their duty of care.

---

#### 2.6 Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance No. 2 of 1937 (FFPO)
*(Amended by Act No. 49 of 1993, Act No. 22 of 2009, Act No. 7 of 2022)*

* Sri Lanka's principal wildlife statute, enforced primarily by the **Department of Wildlife Conservation (DWC)**.
* **Wild Elephants Protection**:
  * Absolute protection for wild elephants. Killing, injuring, shooting, capturing, or possessing a wild elephant without a ministerial permit is a non-bailable, serious criminal offence.
* **Tamed Elephants Registration (Section 22A)**:
  * All captive/tamed elephants must be registered with the DWC and hold an annual license.
  * *Controversy*: The *Extraordinary Gazette No. 2241/41 of 19 August 2021* attempted to regularize irregularly held elephants, sparking widespread public-interest litigation by animal welfare groups (e.g. RARE, JAN) challenging the illegal capture and laundering of wild elephant calves.
* **Sanctuaries & National Parks**:
  * Hunting, capturing, harassing animals, or destroying natural habitats within National Parks and Sanctuaries carries heavy fines and mandatory prison terms.

#### 2.7 Rabies Ordinance No. 7 of 1893 & Dogs Registration Ordinance No. 25 of 1901
* **Rabies Ordinance**: Historically permitted culling of stray dogs suspected of rabies.
* **2006 Presidential Directive (No-Kill Policy)**: Formally suspended the killing of street dogs in favour of **CNVR (Catch-Neuter-Vaccinate-Release)**, making humane mass vaccination and sterilization the official state policy.
* **Elimination of Rabies and Registration of Dogs Bill (2026 Proposed)**:
  * Repeals the 1893 and 1901 colonial ordinances.
  * Mandates annual rabies vaccination and local authority registration for all dogs over 6 weeks.
  * Introduces microchipping, regulates commercial dog breeding establishments, and mandates CNVR programs for all local councils.

#### 2.8 Penal Code of Sri Lanka (Sections on Animal Harm)
* **Section 282 (Negligent conduct with respect to an animal)**:
  * Whoever knowingly or negligently omits to take such order with any animal in his possession as is sufficient to guard against any probable danger to human life, or any probable danger of grievous hurt from such animal, shall be punished with imprisonment up to 6 months, or with a fine up to Rs. 100, or with both.
* **Section 411 (Mischief by killing or maiming animal of value Rs. 10)**:
  * Whoever commits mischief by killing, poisoning, maiming, or rendering useless any animal or animals of the value of Rs. 10 or upwards, shall be punished with imprisonment up to 2 years, or with fine, or both.
* **Section 412 (Mischief by killing or maiming cattle, horse, elephant, etc.)**:
  * Whoever commits mischief by killing, poisoning, maiming, or rendering useless any elephant, camel, horse, mule, buffalo, bull, cow, or ox, or any other animal of the value of Rs. 50 or upwards, shall be punished with imprisonment up to **5 years**, or with fine, or both.
  * *(Note: Framed as property damage against an owner, which is problematic for unowned street animals, but provides serious criminal sanctions for owned companion animals and livestock).*

| :--- | :--- |

### 4. Landmark Judicial Precedents & Case Law Analysis

#### 1. Madawela v. Rawther (1914) 17 NLR 302
* **Facts**: A fish vendor in Puttalam kept a live captured Dugong (marine mammal) without food, inflicting bodily mutilation to sell pieces of flesh while alive.
* **Defense**: The accused argued a wild sea creature is not an "animal" under the 1907 Ordinance.
* **Held (De Sampayo, J.)**: Once any wild or free creature is captured and brought into human custody, it becomes a "captive animal" protected by the 1907 Ordinance. Starving and mutilating a captured creature is a punishable crime.

#### 2. Jayasinghe v. Silva (1921) 23 NLR 28
* **Principle**: **Vicarious Liability of Animal Owners**.
* **Facts**: An owner of 18 bullock carts was prosecuted after SPCA inspectors found a carter driving a bull with an open, infected neck wound. The owner pleaded he gave general oral orders not to work sick animals.
* **Held (De Sampayo, J.)**: Giving general verbal instructions to employees is *not* a valid defense. An animal owner has an **absolute, non-delegable duty** of active supervision. The master is criminally liable for the carter's cruelty.

#### 3. Inspector SPCA Nawalapitiya v. Punchirala (1921) 24 NLR 202
* **Principle**: Protection of Wild Animals from Sadistic Violence.
* **Facts**: Villagers cornered a wild elk and beat it to death with clubs in an excessively cruel manner.
* **Held (Schneider, J.)**: The word "animal" in the 1907 Ordinance extends to wild animals subjected to human cruelty. While lawful hunting is recognized, surrounding and clubbing a wild animal to death constitutes illegal cruelty under Section 2.

#### 4. Mendis v. Hunucumbura (1928) 29 NLR 380
* **Principle**: Illegal Retaliation Against Trespassing Animals.
* **Facts**: A land owner shot a neighbour's cow in the leg after it trespassed onto his land.
* **Held (Garvin, J.)**: Shooting an animal when peaceful expulsion was possible constitutes "causing unnecessary pain" under Section 4(1)(b) of the 1907 Ordinance. Trespassing does not give a license for cruelty.

#### 5. Badulla High Court Revision Case No. Rev. 41/2017
* **Principle**: **Interim Custody of Cruelty Victims to Welfare NGOs**.
* **Held**: Where animals have been subjected to cruelty or illegal transport, Magistrates have the jurisdiction to hand over interim custody to a registered Animal Welfare Organization on a bond pending trial, rather than keeping them in police custody or releasing them back to the abuser.

#### 6. Supreme Court Appeal No. 100/2025
*(Justice for Animals and Nature v. OIC Udawalawa Police and Others)*
* **Crucial Ruling**: 16 water buffaloes were discovered packed cruelly into an open lorry. The offenders pleaded guilty in the Magistrate's Court. The Magistrate subsequently ordered the buffaloes returned to the convicted offenders.
* **Held by the Supreme Court**: The Magistrate's order releasing the victim animals back to the perpetrators was **unlawful and set aside**. The court held that releasing animals back to individuals who admitted treating them cruelly, without an independent welfare evaluation, undermines the entire purpose of cruelty legislation.

#### 7. Court of Appeal: CA (PHC) APN 22/2018
*(Irangani De Silva & Others v. OIC Police Station Ella & Mohomed Aniz)*
* **Principles Established**:
  1. **Strict Ownership Proof**: Under the Animals Act, possession alone is meaningless; valid **Cattle Vouchers** signed before the GVS are mandatory.
  2. **Locus Standi for Welfare Organizations**: Animal welfare organizations have a legitimate legal interest and locus standi to intervene in court proceedings to protect seized animals.

### 5. The Animal Welfare Bill (2026): Modernization, Duty of Care & Gaps

The Animal Welfare Bill represents the most comprehensive overhaul of Sri Lankan animal law since 1907.

```text
┌─────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1907 ORDINANCE                  │ 2026 ANIMAL WELFARE BILL        │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┤
│ Maximum fine: Rs. 100/=         │ Fines up to Rs. 500,000/=       │
│ Imprisonment: Max 3-6 months    │ Imprisonment up to 3 years      │
│ Narrow list of prohibited acts  │ Statutory "Duty of Care"        │
│ No dedicated enforcement body   │ National Animal Welfare Auth.   │
│ 3-month prosecution limit       │ Modern criminal procedure       │
│ Animals treated as property     │ Recognized as sentient beings   │
└─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Core Innovations of the 2026 Bill:
1. **Statutory Duty of Care**: Requires every owner or person in charge of an animal to ensure its basic welfare needs are met (nutrition, suitable environment, disease prevention, and normal behavioural expression). Chronic neglect is now directly actionable.
2. **National Animal Welfare Authority (NAWA)**: Dedicated institutional authority with authorized inspectors empowered to inspect breeding facilities, pet shops, and transport operations.
3. **Clause 47 Enumerated Powers**: 20 specific regulatory fields, including commercial pet shops, dog breeding regulation, animal transport, and blood sports.

#### Critical Gaps Identified by Civil Society:
* **Slaughterhouse Regulation**: Clause 47 does not explicitly regulate abattoir welfare standards, leaving meat processing under public health rather than humane welfare oversight.
* **Authority Governance Dilution**: Reduced animal-welfare-society nominees from 6 (in 2006 draft) to 5 on an advisory committee.
* **Captive Elephant Protections**: Tension between traditional perahera pageant demands and international welfare science regarding chaining, bullhooks, and wild capture.

### 6. Historical, Cultural & Religious Heritage

Sri Lanka possesses one of the world's most ancient traditions of animal protection:
- **World's First Wildlife Sanctuary (3rd Century BCE)**: Following the arrival of Arahant Mahinda, King Devanampiyatissa established the **Mihintale Sanctuary**, embodying the Buddhist principle: *"The birds of the air and the beasts have as equal a right to live and move about any part of this land as thou. The land belongs to the people and all living beings; thou art only the guardian of it."*
- **Ma-Ghata (Royal Decrees of Non-Slaughter)**:
  - King Amanda Gamani (20–30 CE), Voharika Tissa (209–231 CE), Silakala (518–531 CE), and Aggabodhi IV (658–674 CE) completely banned the killing of animals throughout the realm.
  - King Nissanka Malla (12th Century) banned animal slaughter within a 7-gawwa radius of cities and protected fish in 12 great reservoirs.
- **King Buddhadasa (4th Century CE)**: Established veterinary clinics and animal hospitals nationwide, providing medical treatment to wildlife, horses, and elephants.
- **King Elara's Bell of Justice (204–164 BCE)**: When the King's chariot ran over a calf, the mother cow rang the palace justice bell; the king ordered his son punished to uphold equal justice for animals.
- **Four Brahma Viharas (Buddhist Core)**:
  1. *Metta*: Universal loving-kindness to all creatures.
  2. *Karuna*: Compassionate action to relieve suffering.
  3. *Mudita*: Altruistic joy in others' well-being.
  4. *Upekkha*: Equanimous balance free from aversion.
- **Universal Multi-Faith Support**:
  - *Hinduism*: Reverence of Gomata (cow), Nandi, Ganesha, and the doctrine of Ahimsa.
  - *Islam*: Teachings of Prophet Muhammad forbidding cruelty, animal fighting, branding faces, or separating mother birds from chicks; rewarding the quenching of a thirsty dog.
  - *Christianity*: St. Francis of Assisi's *Canticle of the Creatures*; Proverbs 12:10 (*"A righteous man cares for the needs of his animal"*).

### 7. Operational Guide: Filing Complaints & Magistrate Court Procedures

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   AWC CITIZEN & POLICE INTERVENTION WORKFLOW                   │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. INCIDENT DISCOVERY & EVIDENCE PRESERVATION                                  │
│    • Record photos/video showing clear context, date, and location.            │
│    • Note vehicle registration #, driver details, and witness contacts.        │
│    • Check: Is the incident within the 3-MONTH limitation period (1907 Act)?  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. POLICE LODGEMENT (Local Station / 119)                                      │
│    • Request to record a formal complaint under Prevention of Cruelty to       │
│      Animals Ordinance No. 13 of 1907 (Section 2, 3, 4, or 5).                 │
│    • Remind the officer: This is a COGNIZABLE OFFENCE under Section 12         │
│      (Power to arrest without a warrant).                                      │
│    • Cite IGP Circular No. 2024/2007 if cattle transport/slaughter is involved.│
│    • Obtain the Police Complaint Reference Number (CIB Entry #).               │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. MAGISTRATE COURT PROCEEDINGS (B-Report)                                     │
│    • Police file a B-Report to report facts to the Magistrate.                 │
│    • If animals are seized, invoke Badulla HC Rev 41/2017 & CA APN 22/2018:   │
│      Request Magistrate to grant temporary care/custody to a registered NGO    │
│      on a bond, preventing return to the abuser (SC Appeal No. 100/2025).      │
│    • If cattle transport lacks valid DS Permit, GVS Health Cert, or Ear Tags,  │
│      move for confiscation of the transport vehicle (Nizar v. SI Kirinda).     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
