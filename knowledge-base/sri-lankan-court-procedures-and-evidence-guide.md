### 1. The Anatomy of a Criminal Case in the Magistrate's Court

In Sri Lanka, crimes involving cruelty to animals, illegal slaughter, or dangerous animals are adjudicated primarily in the **Magistrate's Court (මහේස්ත්‍රාත් අධිකරණය)** or the **Primary Court (ප්‍රාථමික අධිකරණය)** (under Section 33 of the Judicature Act).

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                          SRI LANKAN CRIMINAL JUSTICE PROCESS                            │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Incident Occurs ➔ 2. Police Complaint (CIB/IB) ➔ 3. Police Investigation           │
│                                                               │                        │
│                                                               ▼                        │
│ 6. Trial & Verdict  ◀── 5. Bail & Charges  ◀── 4. Initial B-Report Filed in Court     │
│    (Fines / Jail)        (Sec 115 / Bail Act)     (Case Number Issued by Magistrate)   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. What is a "B-Report"? (B-වාර්තාව / 'B' அறிக்கை)

#### Definition
A **B-Report** (submitted under **Section 115 and Section 136(1)(b) of the Code of Criminal Procedure Act No. 15 of 1979**) is the formal written report that the Police submit to the Magistrate to initiate a criminal proceeding.

#### Why is the B-Report so critical?
1. **Creates the Official Court Case:** Before a B-Report is filed, an incident is merely an internal police complaint in the Information Book (IB). The moment the police file the B-Report, the Magistrate assigns an official **Case Number** (e.g., `B/1234/2026`).
2. **Empowers Legal Representation:** Once a case number exists, the complainant, pet owner, or an animal welfare coalition (such as AWC) can retain an **Attorney-at-Law** to file a proxy/motion, access the court case docket, make submissions, and protect victim animals.
3. **Police Powers & Orders:** The police use initial B-Reports to request judicial orders from the Magistrate:
   * Order to dispatch a Government Veterinary Surgeon (GVS) to conduct a post-mortem or medical examination.
   * Search warrant to enter private property or illegal slaughterhouses.
   * Order to impound vehicles used for illegal cattle transport.

#### Initial vs. Subsequent B-Reports
* **Initial B-Report (මූලික B-වාර්තාව):** Summarizes the first complaint, identifies suspected offences (e.g. 1907 Ordinance Sec 2, Penal Code 411), and notifies court that an investigation has commenced.
* **Subsequent / Final B-Report (පසු B-වාර්තාව):** Updates the Magistrate on investigation findings, GVS reports, witness statements, and formally names the accused suspects for summons or trial.

### 3. The Importance of Witness Statements (සාක්ෂිකරුවන්ගේ ප්‍රකාශ)

Under **Section 110 of the Code of Criminal Procedure Act**, police officers conduct investigations by examining persons acquainted with the facts and recording their statements in writing.

#### Why Eyewitness Statements Win Cases:
* **Evidence Beyond Reasonable Doubt:** Sri Lankan criminal law requires proving an accused person guilty beyond reasonable doubt. Hearsay (what someone told you or what circulated on WhatsApp/Facebook) is **inadmissible** in court.
* **Direct Testimony:** Courts require an individual who saw, heard, or witnessed the act with their own eyes to provide a sworn statement.
* **Credibility & Consistency:** What you say in your initial statement at the police station will be compared against what you testify under oath in the witness box.

#### Best Practices for Giving a Police Statement:
1. **Stick strictly to direct observations:** Say *"I saw the accused strike the dog three times with a wooden club"*, NOT *"The accused is an evil man who always hates dogs"*.
2. **Record exact times, dates, and locations:** Mention lighting conditions, distance, and landmarks.
3. **Identify the suspect clearly:** If known by name, state it; if unknown, describe clothing, physical features, height, and vehicle license plate numbers.
4. **Read Before Signing:** You have the legal right to read the recorded statement (or have it read aloud to you) to ensure the officer accurately typed or wrote what you stated before signing.

### 4. How to Submit Audio & Video Evidence (3 CDs & Affidavit)

In today's digital era, mobile phone footage and CCTV are the most powerful proof in animal abuse cases. However, Sri Lankan courts do not accept unverified smartphone screens or raw USB drives casually handed over.

Under the **Evidence (Special Provisions) Act, No. 14 of 1995**, electronic recordings (audio, video, CCTV) must follow a strict legal submission protocol:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                SUBMITTING ELECTRONIC EVIDENCE (THE 3-CD RULE)          │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. THREE (03) IDENTICAL COMPACT DISCS (CD-R or DVD-R):                │
│    • Disc 1: For the Court Record (for the Magistrate).                │
│    • Disc 2: For the Prosecution (for the Police / State Counsel).     │
│    • Disc 3: For the Defense (served to the Accused's Lawyer).         │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. SWORN AFFIDAVIT (දිවුරුම් ප්‍රකාශය) SIGNED BY RECORDER / EXTRACTOR:  │
│    • Signed before a Justice of the Peace (JP) or Commissioner.        │
│    • Identifies device make, model, and serial number.                 │
│    • States date, time, and exact GPS / street location of recording.  │
│    • Swears that the recording is authentic, unedited, and unaltered.  │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Required Contents of the Accompanying Affidavit:
1. **Identity of the Recorder:** Full name, NIC number, and address of the person who filmed the video or extracted the CCTV.
2. **Device Details:** Smartphone model, camera make, or CCTV DVR serial number used.
3. **Authenticity Statement:** A solemn declaration under oath that the file on the discs is an **exact, unaltered, continuous replica** of the original recording, free from artificial filtering, cuts, splicing, or audio dubbing.
4. **Date & Context:** Time, date, and location where the video was captured.
5. **Burning Process:** Stating that the deponent personally burned the files onto 3 new CD-R discs on a specified computer.

### 5. Notice vs. Summons vs. Warrant (දැන්වීම්, සිතාසි, වරෙන්තු)

Understanding the legal hierarchy of court and police notices is essential:

| Instrument | Sinhala / Tamil | Legal Meaning & Enforceability | What Happens Upon Failure to Comply? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Notice** | දැන්වීම / அறிவித்தல் | An administrative request issued by police or a committee asking a citizen to attend a station or meeting for inquiry. | Not a judicial command. However, ignoring a police notice may prompt police to obtain a judicial summons. |
| **Summons** | සිතාසිය / அழைப்பாணை | A **formal judicial command** issued under the signature and seal of a Magistrate (Sections 44–50 of the CCPA) ordering a person to appear in court on a specific date and time. | Failure to appear without valid medical/legal excuse results in the immediate issuance of a **Warrant of Arrest**. |
| **Warrant of Arrest** | අත්අඩංගුවට ගැනීමේ වරෙන්තුව / பிடிவிறாந்து | A **court order** directing law enforcement officers to arrest the named individual anywhere in the country and produce them before the Magistrate (Sections 51–62 of the CCPA). | Accused is taken into physical custody. May be Bailable (released upon bond) or Non-Bailable (remanded to prison). |
| **Search Warrant** | සෝදිසි වරෙන්තුව / சோதனை பிடிவிறாந்து | A judicial order authorizing police to enter private homes, farms, or facilities to seize evidence, weapons, or captive animals. | Obstructing an officer executing a search warrant is a criminal offence. |

### 6. Court Exhibits & Productions (නඩු භාණ්ඩ)

Under **Section 425 of the Code of Criminal Procedure Act**, all physical objects connected to a crime are seized as **Court Exhibits (නඩු භාණ්ඩ)**:

* **In Animal Cruelty Cases:**
  * **Weapons / Instruments:** Sticks, iron rods, shovels, bullhooks (හෙන්ඩුව), chains, traps, or poisons.
  * **Vehicles:** Trucks, lorries, or vans used for illegal cattle transport (subject to state confiscation under *Nizar v. SI Kirinda CA 861/85*).
  * **Victim Animals:** Live animals or deceased bodies (subject to post-mortem).
* **The Living Animal Dilemma:**
  * Living animals cannot be locked in police evidence rooms like knives or guns!
  * Under **Badulla High Court Rev. 41/2017** and **CA (PHC) APN 22/2018**, animal welfare lawyers can move the Magistrate to release seized live animals into the **temporary care and custody of a recognized animal welfare organization** on an indemnity bond, rather than returning them to the abuser (*SC Appeal No. 100/2025*).

### 7. Citizen's Guide to the Right to Information (RTI) Act No. 12 of 2016

Sri Lanka's **Right to Information Act No. 12 of 2016** is one of the strongest civic accountability laws in South Asia. Animal advocates can use RTI to access state records, uncover cruelty cover-ups, and audit public animal welfare spending.

#### What Public Authorities Can You RTI?
* **Sri Lanka Police Department:** Obtain certified copies of Information Book entries, complaints, and B-Reports.
* **Department of Animal Production and Health (DAPH):** Veterinary quarantine logs, livestock movement permits, animal health certificates.
* **Department of Wildlife Conservation (DWC):** Captive elephant registration records, permit files, wildlife autopsy reports.
* **Local Authorities (Municipal / Urban / Pradeshiya Sabhas):** Butcher licenses, dog sterilization budgets, municipal shelter records.
* **Ministry of Health:** Rabies vaccination expenditures, human vaccine procurement budgets.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE 4-STEP RTI APPLICATION PROCESS                   │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Step 1: Submit Form RTI 01 to the Information Officer (IO)             │
│ • State precisely what information / certified copies you need.        │
│ • Submit by hand (get stamped receipt) or registered post.             │
│ • Statutory deadline: IO must respond within 14 WORKING DAYS.         │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Step 2: First Appeal to the Designated Officer (DO) (Form RTI 10)       │
│ • If IO refuses, fails to respond, or charges unreasonable fees.       │
│ • File within 30 days of the IO's refusal.                             │
│ • Statutory deadline: DO must decide within 21 WORKING DAYS.          │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Step 3: Second Appeal to RTI Commission (RTIC) (Form RTI 11)           │
│ • Independent appellate body in Colombo.                               │
│ • Can summon public officials, order disclosure, and penalize officers.│
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
